Case Study Project

Text-file processing with Python

Your goal

Develop your solution in Jupyter Notebook or Google Colab, using Python to process three related CSV text files. Treat the files as related tables: use identifiers to find records and combine information across files, then answer useful business questions.

Work in a team of 2–3 students. Your solution must use functions, appropriate collections, explicit loops and conditions, text-file handling, and error handling.

Organize one notebook into clearly labelled sections for loading the data, maintaining customer records, running the assigned analyses, and demonstrating the results.

How to register your team

1. Form a team of 2–3 students, preferably from groups G01–G02.
Optional: teammates from other groups

You may also invite students from groups G03–G06. Please agree on this with the instructor of each invited student’s practical group first.

Then email me the invited students’ names and Neptun codes so I can arrange their dashboard passwords. They will need these passwords to accept the team invitation.

The team founder must be from G01 or G02.

2. Choose your founder.

Their practical group determines the team’s topic. For example, a G02 student who joins a G01 founder’s team will work on Equipment rental, not Webshop.

3. The founder creates the team.

Open the registration page, enter your own Neptun code and dashboard password, then enter the team name and the Neptun codes of one or two teammates.

4. Every invited member accepts separately.

Each teammate opens the registration page with their own Neptun code and dashboard password and selects Accept invitation. Invitation emails are not sent; the founder should notify the invited members.

5. The founder checks and finalizes the team.

All 2–3 members must show Accepted before the founder selects Finalize team registration. Use Refresh team status to see recent acceptances.

6. Access your assigned project when assignment release opens.

After finalization and release, your team will be able to access its task variant, complete dataset, and submission instructions. Each member will use their own credentials.

Before you finalize

You may have several pending invitations, but you can be an accepted member of only one team. Do not share passwords. While a team is a draft, the founder can edit it and members can leave.

Topic overview: G01 — Equipment rental

You are helping a small equipment-rental business understand its rentals, customers, and equipment use. Depending on your assigned variant, your work may focus on late returns, revenue, customer activity, or availability.

The data model

File One row represents Main identifiers
equipment.csv One individually identifiable equipment item equipment_id
customers.csv One customer customer_id
rentals.csv One rental of one equipment item by one customer rental_id, equipment_id, customer_id

Each rental refers to an existing customer and equipment item. One customer can have several rentals; one equipment item can be rented several times at different times.

Small illustrative data example — not the assigned dataset

equipment.csv

equipment_id;name;category;daily_fee_huf
E001;Cordless drill;Tools;2000
E002;Projector;Electronics;4500
E003;Camping tent;Outdoor;3000

customers.csv

customer_id;name;city;customer_type
C001;Anna Kovacs;Budapest;regular
C002;Ben Nagy;Gyor;student
C003;City Events;Budapest;business

rentals.csv

rental_id;customer_id;equipment_id;start_day;due_day;return_day
R001;C001;E001;1;4;5
R002;C002;E002;6;9;
R003;C001;E003;8;10;10

For this project, dates are represented by integer day numbers so that you can concentrate on Python algorithms. An empty return_day means that the equipment has not been returned. Exact calculation rules will be included in your assigned task.

For example, to display a rental together with a customer’s name and an equipment item’s daily fee, you need information from all three files.

Types of questions

Examples include identifying overdue rentals, calculating fees, finding equipment with little use, comparing customer activity, and producing ranked reports. These are examples of the topic, not a list of functions that every team must implement.

Topic overview: G02 — Webshop

You are helping a small webshop analyze its products, customers, and purchases. Depending on your assigned variant, your work may focus on pending orders, product performance, customer activity, or category performance.

The data model

File One row represents Main identifiers
products.csv One product product_id
customers.csv One customer customer_id
order_items.csv One product line within an order order_id + line_no, product_id, customer_id

An order can contain several lines. Lines with the same order_id belong to the same order, so the number of rows is not necessarily the number of orders. The combination of order_id and line_no identifies a line.

Small illustrative data example — not the assigned dataset

products.csv

product_id;name;category;unit_price_huf;stock
P001;Notebook;Stationery;900;25
P002;Desk lamp;Home;6500;8
P003;USB cable;Electronics;1800;30

customers.csv

customer_id;name;city;customer_type
C001;Anna Kovacs;Budapest;regular
C002;Ben Nagy;Gyor;student
C003;City Events;Budapest;business

order_items.csv

order_id;line_no;customer_id;product_id;quantity;unit_price_huf;order_day;status
O001;1;C001;P001;2;900;3;paid
O001;2;C001;P003;1;1800;3;paid
O002;1;C002;P002;1;6500;7;pending

The first two rows form one order, with a value of 2 × 900 + 1 × 1800 = 3600 HUF. Order-line prices record the price at the time of ordering; they may differ from the current product price.

The project uses integer day numbers and integer HUF amounts. The assigned task will explain the treatment of paid, pending, and cancelled orders.

Types of questions

Examples include calculating pending order values, measuring product revenue, identifying repeat customers, finding products without paid sales, and comparing categories. Your assigned variant will specify the exact questions and required functions.

What every team must build

1. Load and connect the files
  • Read the three supplied CSV files using UTF-8 encoding and the ; delimiter. Skip the header and convert numeric fields to numbers.
  • Store the records in appropriate Python collections, such as lists of dictionaries.
  • Use identifiers to connect related records. Do not rely on matching row positions across files.
  • Handle missing files and invalid data with understandable messages. Keep the original supplied dataset unchanged.
2. Maintain customer records

Implement customer Create, Read, Update, and Delete (CRUD) operations:

  • add a new customer with a unique identifier;
  • find and display a customer by identifier;
  • update a customer’s name, city, or type, while keeping the identifier unchanged;
  • delete a customer only if no rental or order line refers to that customer.

Save customer changes to a separate output file, then demonstrate that the saved file can be read again. Do not require the user to edit the CSV manually.

3. Answer four assigned business questions

Your team will receive four analysis functions, including a ranking/report function. The tasks will involve several of the basic algorithms studied in class: searching, summarization, counting, selection, copying/transformation, separation, or sorting.

At least two assigned analyses will combine information from multiple files. The task specifications will identify which records to include, how to calculate results, and how to handle ties or empty results.

4. Demonstrate and export the results
  • Use clearly labelled notebook cells to demonstrate all required operations.
  • Use meaningful prompts, headings, and output labels. Exact decorative wording is not assessed.
  • Export at least one assigned report to a separate CSV or text file with clear column names or labels.
  • Keep calculation functions separate from keyboard input and displayed output. Return values from those functions so that they can be tested independently.
5. Notebook structure and reproducibility

Your notebook must run from top to bottom in a fresh Jupyter or Google Colab session using only the three supplied CSV files as external input.

  • Create all additional small test datasets within notebook cells. File-handling tests must create their own temporary CSV files in a separate test directory; reports and other output files must also be generated by the notebook.
  • Do not modify or overwrite the supplied CSV files. Do not depend on extra uploaded files, a personal Google Drive location, files from another computer, or variables left over from a previous session.
  • Keep function definitions before their use. Use clear headings for data loading, CRUD operations, the four analyses, tests, and report export.
  • Before submission, restart the kernel/runtime and run all cells in order. All cells must finish without an unhandled error. Intentionally invalid test cases should catch and explain the expected exception rather than stop execution.
  • Use the three input CSVs from one documented working directory. Explain how to place or upload them in the PDF documentation.

Submit the downloaded .ipynb file, including when working in Colab or in Jupyter.

Function specifications and implementation rules

View function specifications and implementation rules

Your assigned task will provide a compact specification for each required function:

Item What will be specified
Function name The exact name to define
Parameters Their names, types, and meanings
Returns The type and meaning of the returned value
Rules Included records, calculation rules, ordering, ties, and empty results
Example A small call with its expected result

Use structured algorithms with explicit loops and conditions. For functions intended to demonstrate a basic algorithm, do not replace the algorithm with sum(), min(), max(), sorted(), .sort(), any(), all(), .index(), or .count().

Do not use comprehensions, generator expressions, lambda, map(), filter(), break, continue, or a return statement to leave a loop early. Normal function return statements after loops are allowed. Type annotations are optional.

Ordinary collection operations, including indexing, dictionary lookup, .items(), .append(), and set membership, may be used where appropriate. Do not use import statements, including imports from the Python standard library. Read and write the supplied simple semicolon-separated CSV files using open(), readline(), explicit loops, split() and write(). Do not use the csv module, pandas, NumPy, a database engine, or other imported modules in the notebook code.

Object-oriented programming is not required. Prefer clear functions and simple data structures over unnecessary complexity.

Testing and individual understanding

View testing and individual requirements

For each of the four analysis functions, provide at least three tests with inputs and expected results:

  • a normal case;
  • an empty/no-match case, where valid for that function;
  • a boundary or special case, such as equal values, a threshold, or an order with several lines.

If a function requires non-empty data, test its documented response to an invalid empty input rather than inventing a result. Also demonstrate duplicate-ID rejection, rejection of a missing referenced identifier during data loading, and protection against deleting a referenced customer.

For each test, display or check the expected result and actual result, and explain any difference. Printing a value without stating what should have been returned is not a complete test. Include tests and their results in the notebook.

Each member must understand the shared solution. During the project presentation, each member should be able to explain a function, trace it on a few records, and make a small change to a requirement. Describe each member’s contribution in the PDF documentation.

Use of AI assistance

Use AI assistance to support understanding, explain errors, or discuss your own code. Do not submit a complete generated project as if it were your own work.

Include a brief AI-use statement in the PDF documentation describing what assistance you used and how you checked it, or state that no AI assistance was used. Every team member remains responsible for understanding and testing the submitted solution. Code that you cannot explain or adapt does not demonstrate the required individual understanding.
